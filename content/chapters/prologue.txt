# Siachen Glacier, Bharatvarsh, 2189

“Harvard? That’s so cool! Say Congrats To Nirmay from me…” Lt. Kaushik was an eerie and lively fellow. Recently his superior Maj. Shah’s son was offered a PhD at Harvard.

“Well Kaushik, the world is changing rapidly. The number of Astral Power Users are surging rapidly, their powers unimaginable and unconquered as of yet. The world needs to adapt… find new sources to sustain and survive. I’m glad my son is choosing a path where he will risk his safety for others rather than his own benefit.”

“But Sir, without the Astrals, we are unguarded. Calamities, Hybrids and Entities roam free, and we all know how that went last time one of it hit Kyoto… It took three Astrals working together to bring one of them down.”

“True. But the powers one possesses also make one greed for more… Astral energy is not something to be possessed by humanity, I believe. Protection is a façade, and now we have more of them in militaries instead of harnessing their powers for their true purpose.”

The world, as it was, was divided. Astrals were a topic of debate in every nation, and yet no one was willing to stand against them. For they were the divine’s chosen ones to harness the power, protect us, nurture us…

But power comes in both ways… it influences, pierces the mind as a double-edged sword. And so was the case with Astrals.

***

The winds over Siachen were calm that day… an air of uneasiness was looming, and although such weather patterns were incredibly normal to the point that even the hound dogs didn’t bother, something didn’t feel right today.

# At Radar Point 1001

“Is that…?” Lt. Kiran was spooked by a slight blink.

“What’s wrong Sehgal? Haven’t had sleep?” These were words from his annoying and indolent colleague, Lt. Samuel Wilson.

“No Sam, I felt a power surge over the east, approximately 35°11’57”. We have to be sure, I will just repor—”

The huge blast tore open the control room’s western wall. And Lt. Kiran’s blood was splattered across the desk and the screens that were still running.

Half the control room was wiped out in mere seconds when the figure loomed in front of them. The haze obscured everyone’s vision, but a figure taller than any other stood inside.

Samuel pointed his gun with half-hearted strength, resolved and attempted to shoot, but was met with a sharp blast of an energy wave splitting his remains in half. Every individual who dared to point a gun was slain like bugs.

A call was placed and was received by the figure approaching.

“This is Northern Command, I repeat, we are sending backup, I repeat we are sending backup… Until then, hold the intruders!”

“Intruders… interesting.”

“Wh… who is speaking?”

“Shan Ziang. You might as well proceed with an entire army… or I might soon visit Delhi myself.”

“Shan, we had an agreement. You don’t need to do this. We… we respect your deal with us… but we want more time.”

“Time… an interesting bargain. But the world doesn’t have time. No one does. Not especially when the Artifact lies with authorities like you, who cannot even protect against a massacre like the one caused by Titan Merwah. The Artifact doesn’t belong to weaklings… Hand it over or see your beloved city turned to mere dust.”

“Shan, we told you the Artifact doesn’t possess any astral weapon… or anything known to possess powers derived from astral sources. It’s merely an ancient pendant! Please believe us, you don’t need to do this!”

“I will believe your words when I see it myself.”

“We humbly request that you take no further steps, or we will fire our Zhargos missiles… Just return to Zhongguo, we will soon hand over the Artifact to the Astral Research League!”

“Really? Well, it has been a long time since I tested Kusarigama’s defensive capabilities… so just do your best, cowards.”

Shan’s Kusarigama was an astral weapon of profound power. So strong that, during the Great Astral Surge, the great Mount Nayamusa was split in half with its energy.

And when the Zhargos missiles were fired, he simply created a powerful light-energy force field which split all incoming missiles in half.

All of Siachen trembled with blasts and a powerful barrage. The ice above collapsed, creating a massive avalanche crushing everything beneath. Outposts nearby were destroyed, tents shattered, and hundreds of soldiers were trapped under the ice.

Yet Shan remained unscathed.

We expected at least a scratch from so massive a bombardment on his body, but when the haze cleared, he emerged out of it as if it were a regular Sunday for him.

# Back in Delhi

Col. Lalwani remained aghast. Fear showed in his eyes, and there was total chaos at headquarters. Officials were panicking as if it were doomsday itself. The chatter was so loud that no one could even hear their own words. Tea cups remained spilled. Half of them were trying to remain optimistic to find ways to stop this monster, and half had already accepted what was coming.

Brig. Naik called his wife:

“Manika, please go. I have to stay here until the end, but you can’t bear the same consequences as me. We have had a good life together… but I can’t drag you into what comes next. I have arranged for your travels… the plane takes off in an hour, Raju will pick you up now… I will always love you, wherever I go.”

“Luv, nothing will happen. I don’t know why you are speaking such words, but I put faith that God will protect us as He always did. I’m not leaving without you…”

“This time, we are facing Hell itself, Mani. No gods, if even one exists, have the ability to stop this devil.”

“Where is Raj?” Lalwani’s voice commanded. For now, his eyes were searching for Bharatvarsh’s last remaining hope.

“He is on his way, Colonel… and so are the rest of the three,” Brig. Naik replied. “Since the attack by Titan Merwah, Raj has been tracking all activity in the Indian Ocean Belt. He also mentioned that he had important business in Leipzig…”

“What? More important than the nation?” the Colonel questioned, growing restless.

“It was about the Artifact, Colonel. Vetramani said it wasn’t ordinary, to say the least.”

“I specifically told him to leave that museum piece behind! And yet all he cares about is the same junk! What about Veraya?”

“Miss Pillai is busy with her family, Colonel. Since the attack… she needed time of her own.”

“Astrals!” Col. Lalwani slammed his fist on the desk where all the officers sat.

“Astrals, what, Colonel?” The voice came from behind as the doors opened.

Raj Vetramani stood with his team, all of them carrying a distinctive aura.

As soon as they entered, a deep silence fell over the room. Because you were not talking about ordinary men or women—these were Astrals.

Vetramani was cold. Literally. An ice user of Astral energy. His trident could freeze even the driest and hottest places.

Don’t let this make you think that was his whole personality, though. For Astrals, he might strike you in the face without hesitation… but for mere humans, he carried warmth everywhere. He treated kids like his own. Whenever a battle struck a poor community, he brought gifts to the children afterward. Tended the wounded like his own family. Hell, once the man paid for an entire building because he couldn’t save it from destruction.

On his left stood his trusted colleague, the beautiful Veraya Pillai. If you say Raj was the leader and the brains, Veraya was the heart of Bharatvarsh. A model when not battling, but don’t let that distract you from the fact that she was fierce. You might prefer facing Hell instead of her… because this flower had thorns. Or acid, rather.

Then came Prayan and Samba. Magma and earth. One could burn through flesh; the other could bury you alive.

“This isn’t some titan, Vetramani… Shan is a menace. The Philippines, Korea, Myanmar… I don’t have to remind you again. You know what he does,” the Colonel said after seeing him.

“Lalwani, attacking him with Zhargos was foolish. An Astral has enough energy to generate barriers with their powers. And he is no naive schoolboy who would just stand there. This only feeds his ego more.”

“We had no choice! We had to use our options… We know it is a losing battle.”

“Raj, can you stop him? Do we stand any chance?” Veraya asked, growing increasingly anxious.

“Shan is clearly more experienced when it comes to battle. He has been here since the Great Astral Surge, long before most of us were even born. It is going to require more from all of us,” Raj replied.

“Oh c’mon! Four Astrals can’t stop him? And you are saying these words? Have you been losing it all on drinking lately?” Prayan was getting agitated by the lack of optimism in the room. “We have the best air squadrons, one of the best armies, and the biggest Astral user in the country is cowering like a rat hiding from its prey!”

“Prayan, you have no idea. His Kusarigama isn’t even our biggest fear… his Astral Form is. No one in the world—no hybrid, no titan, no mere mortal or Astral has ever laid a finger on Shan, and if he transforms… I don’t know what my survival chances are.” Raj knew how Prayan would react, but he had to be realistic.

“But so can you! Your own Astral Form is terrifying! We have seen it, we have all been protected by it, and you say that even then we stand no chance? Don’t mock me, Vetramani!” Prayan replied.

“Raj, we are still scarred… Pondicherry pushed our limits,” Veraya said softly. “We distracted Merwah, led him away, but it came at total exhaustion. And yet we feel the intensity of Shan even right now. I’m afraid Prayan is right…”

Samba nodded. They had just battled the titan not long ago.

“A titan is still a titan, Samba… A human is unpredictable. This Artifact… I was in Leipzig. It looks ordinary, but I’m sure it contains something humanity hasn’t discovered yet.”

“Oh c’mon, Veraya, explain it to him!” Col. Lalwani interrupted. “All scientists, all engineers, all experiments… this junk only has fancy covering! An alien with three eyes and wings that don’t exist imprinted on it, with just a locket that my kid could show off to his rich friends! Nothing more! Get your head out of it!”

“Colonel, if he feels it’s important, and Shan wants it so badly, it’s better to keep it away from Shan’s hands,” Veraya countered.

“Veraya, are you blinded by loyalty? My scientists are no fools. Shan is not after this junk. He is after you!”

“Exactly, Vetramani,” Prayan argued. “You know how much we matter, how much Astrals are required for a nation. We die, and he can have his way! Hybrids, titans, and all those monsters destroy cities, leaving us financially crippled… or worse, he replaces us with his own underlings!”

“Prayan… I want you to trust me. I feel the Artifact as if it is trying to tell me something. Besides…”

Raj presented the Artifact to everyone around him.

“Take it away. I know we can’t prove anything now, and this piece produces no information. But I believe it is no coincidence that a titan had it in its possession. Bury it or destroy it… but right now, I feel it must not land in the hands of Shan. His desperation speaks far more than the Artifact has revealed to us.”

“And what about you? Don’t… this is not Pondicherry. I’m not taking this risk again,” Veraya said, knowing what he was about to do.

“I have no choice. If any of you make it past… maybe we have a chance to rebuild again. We need some of you. So don’t consider my plight, just leave.”

“That gives you no right to face a monster alone, Raj! I’m not placing my bets, no matter how important the Artifact is to you… You are not facing him alone!”

“Veraya, please, listen to me… please.” Tears began to flow, and for the first time, the man who brought smiles to everyone else was losing his composure. “The nation must exist. But for that, you need to live. You all need to live. Think about Subhadra, Veraya… think about your mom. I have nothing else to lose. You all are everything I have. Promise me… you will tell this tale to your kids when you survive.”

“Sure…”

Veraya snatched the Artifact as she rushed into the corridor, clearly tearing up.

“Prayan, take her far from here… and use it if you must. Protect the Artifact at all costs!”

“Playing the hero again, old man… If we die, this is on you!”

Prayan rushed after Veraya, following her downstairs. The Maestro FS 60 roared with supersonic speed along the highway as the three carried the Artifact outside the city.

# The Next Morning: Palam Air Base

Raj stood at Palam Air Base, looking at the photo of an old man.

“I hope I succeed you, big guy…”

“Mr. Vetramani… we have 15 squadrons of X-90s at the base,” a soldier reported. “Maj. Naik will handle the situation, but…”

“I know what you’re thinking, soldier. It’s suicide for the so-called greatest warrior the country has. But I’m not entirely perfect. There was a time when I was unlike you all, soldier. A time when sacrifice was an alien word.”

“Sir, still, it ain’t fair on your part to die like this…”

“Well, even if I fall… they will make it. And I will be honored going down as a martyr rather than what I was before, soldier. Tell the Major to prepare for battle.”

Military squadrons deployed from Palam. The city was evacuated until dawn.

It was a different feeling in Delhi altogether. No one spoke, yet everyone sensed the fear of death. Sweat drops had so much weight that their sound startled the soldiers nearby.

An X-90 radar beeped, sensing a presence.

“Captain, we sense Shan at 12 degrees! Are we clear to engage? I repeat, requesting permission to engage!”

“Permission granted!”

The missiles whistled past the silent air and tore through the winds. But as soon as impact neared, they shattered into dust.

“What…?” Before the pilot could finish, a beam of light pierced the cockpit, exploding the X-90 in midair.

“Towards the east! All units, open fire!”

A massive barrage of missiles filled the eastern sky, and within seconds, each was nullified by the overwhelming presence. Shan was dodging every attack as if it were a high school game of dodgeball.

“How pathetic… You could have done this the easy way, but you people are always so emotional about it. I guess I have no choice left.”

His Kusarigama shone with light as fierce as the sun on a midsummer afternoon. He flung it forward to create a beam of light shattering everything within a twenty-mile radius.

Dust and ashes of fallen X-90s rose into the air. Limbs shattered, metal pierced through the bodies of fallen martyrs. Shan stood atop the wreckage, gleaming proudly over his devilish acts.

“You could have avoided all this bloodshed, Raj. But you minor Astrals never listen.”

“This is not some medieval kingdom, Shan. We sincerely requested time and patience. But your bloodlust has devoured many nations in the east already. I’m not sacrificing mine.”

“Bloodlust, you say? All I heard was a breach of Astral regulations. If the imbalance of power persists, I may have to wipe every one of you out until I remain alone. Consider it a pity, weakling.”

“That doesn’t give you any right to wreak destruction and carnage across the east! Astral forces are meant to protect the weak, to stop the monsters looming over us! And now you are using that power to make nations weak and beg to you! Your puppets are fine where you want them, but I will not tolerate it in my land!”

“Well, it will be fun to kill you.”

Raj charged his trident, creating an icicle storm over Shan. The storm was splintered by Shan’s light field, and Shan swung his Kusarigama toward Raj’s head. The trident blocked the attack as Raj spun out of the way.

Shan charged astral energy into his Kusarigama, unleashing a beam of immense wavelength. Raj dodged using an ice barrier, raising icy rocks around him.

The weapons collided repeatedly as the two Astrals attacked and dodged, each unleashing their best moves.

An exhausted Raj struck his trident into the earth, creating a massive ice storm that raced across the ground, freezing everything in its path toward Shan, who effortlessly dodged once again.

“Give up, weakling. You are no match for me. Give me the Artifact and I shall consider sparing your life.”

“Not yet! You might have seen my best attacks, but I’m not finished yet! And I will keep fighting until my last breath!”

“Very well, I shall grant you the favor.”

Just then, Raj tapped into his deepest Astral powers. An emanating glow broke from his body. A large energy wave surrounded him, creating a mystical barrier of ice… and in seconds, a beast emerged.

Astrals were not ordinary, but Raj wasn’t just a warrior with a magical trident. His powers surpassed any other Astral in the nation. Prayan was perhaps closest, but Raj in this state was on a different level.

Raj emerged from his ice shield, the trident forming a tail of ice behind him. His fangs exhaled freezing air, his eyes glowed sky blue, and his claws were deep enough that a hundred men could stand upon them.

A sharp, cold roar pierced the air, chilling everything in its path.

“I see you have the knowledge to use your Astral Form… Alas, it’s a pity to kill such a magnificent user.”

The beast slammed its foot down, spreading a sheet of ice across the ground. Shan swung his Kusarigama to shatter the ice encasing his feet. Raj summoned a massive hailstorm, but it still broke harmlessly against Shan’s light field. Raj stomped forward, but the Kusarigama broke their contact. The roar intensified as the efforts grew futile.

“Well, let’s show you the true meaning of Astral energy, weakling.”

Shan created a mystical void to harness his transformation into his Astral Form.

Remember why they called it doomsday? You are about to see it now.

Shan’s transformation created a thunderous atmosphere as a gigantic Zhongguan creature roared. A flying dragon appeared out of the clouds, colossal in size. A lightning storm gathered around it. The dragon dove toward the ice beast and slammed him into the ground.

The beast rose, baring its fangs, barely missing the dragon’s tail. Raj swung his own icy tail at the dragon, landing a direct hit that sent the dragon crashing into a nearby building, collapsing it into rubble.

Raj seized the opportunity to pounce, but as soon as he reached, Shan wrapped his dragon coils around Raj’s body, attempting to strangle him to death.

Raj struggled to free himself, but the dragon squeezed tighter. Raj sank his powerful fangs into the dragon’s flesh, causing it to wail in pain. Shan lost his composure and released his grip. The dragon vanished into the clouds.

Anticipating another attack, Raj threw up an ice barrier. Shan suddenly rushed from the side, shattering the barrier, and before Raj could react, Shan fired deadly light waves, brutally injuring the beast.

The beast collapsed, and Raj reverted to his original human form, exhausted and battered. He gathered his remaining strength to face the dragon once more.

Shan fired his bolt beam. The beam struck the ground with tremendous energy, spreading blinding light in every direction. Destruction followed: trees were uprooted, buildings crushed, rubble rained down like an earthquake, and the earth felt as if it were shattering in half.

Vetramani fell, his eyes hoping for a miracle, his breath slowing down.

“I failed you, old man… I failed…”

And with that, he breathed his last.

***

Shan reverted to his human form.

Col. Lalwani sat devastated by the news. He tried to sip his tea, but could not swallow; something felt lodged in his throat as the windows gushed open.

“Where is it?”

“I don’t know… destroyed, perhaps.”

“A country that boasts about righteousness has liars… That is knowledge I will not digest easily. Where is it, Lalwani?”

Shan seized him by the throat. He didn't even need full strength as his fingers choked the life out of the Colonel.

“I… d-don’t… know…”

The lifeless body collapsed into the chair.

“May you be pitied, Colonel.” Shan keyed his comms. “Shan speaking. Get me the coordinates for the woman.”

# National Highway, near Mumbai

“Are we nearing the bunker, Samba? I feel like I lost something deep within me…”

“Ver, he told us not to look back. You know how he puts his own life in danger first. We need to follow his orders.”

“That stupid oaf! If I was there, I wouldn’t have left him to play the hero alone! He acts as if he has no one, when we are right there! Every time, saving his stupid ass from behind… You are awfully quiet, Prayan.”

“Shh… I’m picking up senses.”

“What? How is that possible? This is the latest tech! Its stealth can’t be detected by top military radars! I designed it personally, how can—”

“I’m ejecting.”

“No, Prayan, please!”

“I don’t know whether our hero made it or not, but I have no interest in waiting around to find out!”

“Prayan…” Teary-eyed, Veraya took his hands in hers. “We do this together.”

“Ugh… fine.”

“Using ultraboost, guys. Buckle up!”

The FS 60 tore down the highway like a hellsport. Samba had never driven so fast, using his astral senses to navigate the perilous speed. Other vehicles on the road only saw dust tornadoes screaming past.

Within an hour, the team reached Vasai, 180 miles from prosperous Bombay. The Astrals had a facility equipped for doomsday scenarios. If they could reach it in time, their chances of survival were high.

“No… watch out!”

A light beam struck the left side of the supersonic vehicle, toppling it across the road until it slammed into a peepal tree trunk. The door was flung open. Veraya crawled out bleeding, her forehead bruised and battered.

“Damn you!” Prayan charged the force that had struck them.

His curved swords slashed toward Shan’s face as Shan sidestepped.

“Just like your master… He was weak, and you are weak as well as stupid.”

“He is not my master! What did you do to Raj?”

“He did not comprehend… so I had my ways of dealing with him. Unfortunately, your hero has turned to dust.”

Prayan charged again, only to be knocked back by the Kusarigama’s shield.

“You killed him, you monster!”

“Beautiful lady… perhaps I will spare you if you give that to me.”

“In your dreams, shithead!”

“Such language doesn’t suit someone as beautiful as you.” Shan lifted Veraya by her shirt collar.

“Well, beauty comes with curses, Mr. Shan.”

Her twin darts fired mercury oxide directly onto his skin. Just as he reacted, Veraya kicked him to break free.

As Shan raised his Kusarigama, Samba slammed his bracelets together, creating a massive sand barrier that trapped Shan’s legs.

“Impressive for weaklings like you… but you still don’t stand a chance.”

As Prayan prepared his magma dome, Shan shattered the sand prison and swung his Kusarigama, sending energy bolts to disrupt the incoming assault. All three attacked simultaneously from different angles, but each strike barely touched Shan.

“We cannot defeat him! Pillai, run!”

“What? Are you crazy? He found us! He will find me too!”

“I didn’t say run away from him! A tributary of the Satvati River flows five miles ahead! You know what to do!”

“Whatever your game is, you cannot win against me, weaklings,” Shan scoffed.

“We’ll see about that!” Prayan charged his twin curved swords with fire.

“Didn’t know you had another Astral Form in you… It will be quite a feat to destroy two in a single day.”

“Not so fast, you monster!” Samba locked his entire body into the sand, exerting every ounce of his power to pin Shan down.

***

Veraya used her reflexive speed to flee toward the river.

Behind her, Prayan’s Astral Form erupted: a phoenix of pure magma, incinerating the surrounding forest. Trees blackened instantly, ash and coal filling the sky, the air becoming unbreathable as the earth scorched below.

Shan broke free from the sand as an exhausted Samba collapsed to the side.

“Very well!” The phoenix shrieked as it dove at Shan. The radiant barrier burned as Shan leaped out of the way.

Prayan breathed a torrent of fire. Shan used his Kusarigama to trigger an instant explosion against fallen trees, creating a counter-barrier. The flames breached the barriers, but barely scratched Shan’s personal shield.

Shan leaped through the ashen smoke and landed on the phoenix’s back. The phoenix shrieked, banking violently to throw him off, carrying him high into the sky. But the Kusarigama’s chain latched tightly around the phoenix’s neck.

“Say hello to your master, Prayan.”

Shan fired another light wave point-blank at the phoenix. The blazing bird plummeted toward the earth, crashing in an explosion that decimated everything within radius.

# The Cliff over the Satvati

A bleeding, exhausted Veraya reached the edge of the cliff where the tributary rushed downstream.

“Hello again, my dear… I hope you don’t desire the fate of your imbecile colleagues.”

“Don’t call me dear. Everyone on the planet knows who the real imbecile is.”

“You are a tough one. Give me the Artifact now… maybe we can come to an arrangement.”

“An arrangement? As if you’d let me live.”

“Bengaluru is a beautiful city, isn’t it? Palaces, history, culture… all of your nation’s identity. Not to mention, your family resides there.”

“You monster… You know the consequences if you dare touch them! The Astral League will not leave you alone!”

“A league I established? They have enough strength to go against me?”

“They have enough to put you down!”

“Oh, do they? And what if I tell them that this Artifact is a threat to all of us? That it will allow your pathetic nation to produce more weaklings?”

“It’s a box with a pendant and nothing more!”

“Then give it away. Why gamble the lives of millions for a mere pendant?”

“Raj did not trust you… and neither do I. You will never find the Artifact again!”

“No!”

Veraya clutched the Artifact tightly to her chest and hurled herself off the cliff into the roaring waters below.

Shan stared down into the churning abyss, his eyes blazing.

“Zernon… I need a search team. Retrieve the Artifact.”

“Are you finished with the Astrals, Shan?”

“Of course I am. And I will burn this pathetic nation to the ground.”

“Shan, stand down. We are done. Do not let the box cloud your judgment. Return to base.”

“The Artifact is what I want, Zernon!”

“Shan, that is an order.”

***

The Artifact drifted downstream, floating until it washed ashore at the edge of the city…

Picked up by a man who watched it closely.
