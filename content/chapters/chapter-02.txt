# A Miracle, A Massacre

# Cortis Healthcare Centre, Mumbai, 2204

The rays of the morning sun shone a little brighter through the narrow window sill. Cuckoos cooed their song outside, while across the room in the ward, a bruised and bandaged Abhimanyu tried to gather the strength to greet the birds outside.

“No, you are not getting up,” Aadhya’s voice broke the quiet. Her tone was surprisingly calm, though Abhi had fully expected her to yell at him for his recklessness. “First you ignore all my advice and fight a dozen men alone in an alley, and now you’re ignoring your own body to look at birds?”

Abhimanyu lay still on the hospital bed, his mouth tasting of copper from the previous night’s beatings.

Aadhya lifted the hospital gown to dab iodine onto the scrapes along his ribs.

“Oww… hey, be careful!”

“Not for me to decide that,” she muttered without looking up.

“Where are the others? Did you all stay here the whole night? I hope it didn’t cause too much trou— Ow! Easy, dude!”

“What did you expect? For us to let you die alone in a hospital? Hasan and Kiraj handled all your admission paperwork before heading home. You owe them big time, by the way. Suraj dragged those scuffled goons to the police precinct… although I wonder how long they’ll remember getting beaten up by a twenty-four-year-old engineering student.”

“I’m sorry, Aadhya. I didn’t want you all to worry…”

“Well, we did worry! But luckily for you, it’s just a hairline rib fracture and some scars to prove your bravery. Anyway, I called your mom… and I’ll let her handle the rest.”

“Oh God…”

Minutes later, the screech of tires echoed outside, followed by hurried footsteps down the clinic corridor. Mrs. Devyani Rathore and Saloni burst into the room.

Seeing her son smiling sheepishly through swollen eyes and bandages, the composed demeanor of the mother of two—a woman who had weathered countless hardships without losing her calm—cracked into burning frustration.

“Do you care about us at all, Abhimanyu?” she demanded, her voice trembling.

“Mom, it was a tiny pup… If something had happened to it, how was I supposed to live with myself?”

“And that gives you a reason to fight a dozen street thugs with your bare hands? What if something worse had happened? What about Aadhya? What if Suraj hadn’t arrived in time? Would you have just bled to death in a filthy alley?”

“Mom, I know how to defend myself. I’m not helpless…”

“Stop sounding like your father, Abhimanyu! For once in your life, act with some rationality instead of letting your emotions drag you into danger!”

“Why do you always drag Dad into this? Dad died an honorable man! He lived by his principles!”

“I am not questioning your father’s character, Abhimanyu!” she cried, tears welling in her eyes. “I am talking about consequences! Your father could have retreated, he could have saved himself, but he chose to sacrifice himself for others! And where are all the people who praised his philosophy now? Where were they when we struggled to put food on the table?”

“So you want me to forget everything he taught me? To live as if other people’s trust doesn’t matter?”

“If it keeps you alive, yes! That is the bare minimum I expect from my only son!”

“I’m sorry to disappoint you, Mom… but I would rather die than abandon Dad’s principles.”

“Saloni, talk some sense into him… I’m done here,” Devyani whispered, turning away in tears as she rushed out of the room. Aadhya quickly followed to console her.

Abhi looked toward his sister. “Di…”

Saloni sighed softly, sitting on the edge of the bed. “Maybe she’s right this time, Abhi. Maybe all your kindness isn’t meant for this world. I know how painful that is to hear… but maybe trust just doesn't belong in a place like this anymore.”

“You too? After everything we learned together, you’re the last person I expected to say that.”

“Then consider me the last person,” she said quietly.

***

Three days passed at the clinic. Once the discharge papers were cleared, a fully recovered Abhimanyu rushed straight to Bhojan Darbar. He had missed four consecutive shifts, which meant either double duties, a severe reprimand from the manager, or both.

Panting, he arrived at the restaurant.

Mr. Rana, the manager, caught his eye and gestured toward the alley outside. Customers hurried past, brushing against Abhi without a second glance.

The manager stepped outside, crossing his arms.

“Master Rathore. It seems the humble duties of a kitchen are no longer suited to your refined tastes, and you would prefer to seek employment elsewhere.”

“What? Wait, Mr. Rana, I’m sorry! I was hospitalized, and I called ahead to notify you about my absence—”

“And I have approved your leave, Master Rathore—indefinitely. Now you may rest, or pursue whatever grand scientific endeavors occupy your brilliant mind.”

“Sir, please! I need this job! My college fees, my family’s finances… We’re going through a difficult time!”

“In that case, you have my condolences, young man… but not this job. Now move along and don't make a scene. My patrons have little patience for beggars.”

“Mr. Rana, please! Just give me one more chance!” Tears welled in Abhi’s eyes as the reality crashed down upon him.

“Promises don't balance my ledger, kid. Now scoot. Shoo!”

The kitchen door clicked shut.

***

Evening settled over the city, the dim amber glow of streetlamps casting long shadows across the pavement. Abhi sat on a concrete bench near the park, his head in his hands.

Had he lost everything?

No job to pay his tuition. His research redirected toward anti-Astral defense projects he had no faith in. Was he foolish for clinging to his father’s beliefs?

His phone buzzed in his pocket. Expecting more bad news, he answered reluctantly.

“Master Rathore?”

“Yes, Professor Hans…”

“Your grant proposal for the Gravitational Time Dilator has been approved! The Ministry of Science wants a working prototype demonstrated for their inspection committee! I presented your theoretical framework, and they were extraordinarily impressed!”

Abhi leaped to his feet, his exhaustion evaporating. “Really, Professor? Should I come to the lab right now?”

“Calm down, young man! The review is next week. I am traveling to the government research facility in Mysore the day after tomorrow to secure the neutronium sample. Do not go getting yourself into any more street brawls until then!”

“I won't, Professor! I promise!”

Hanging up, Abhi felt the weight lift from his shoulders. The world that had seemed doomed moments ago was brimming with possibility once more.

***

# University of Excellence: The Breakthrough

The laboratory echoed with the hum of high-voltage transformers and the whir of laser cutters. Abhimanyu worked with manic enthusiasm, his calculations flying across holographic displays.

“Take it easy, Brainiac,” laughed Kushal from a neighboring bench. “The rest of us have projects to finish too!”

“Sorry, Kushal, I’m just ecstatic! Everything has to be calculated to the sub-atomic decimal… Wait, where did my notebook go?”

Abhi scrambled around the room.

“Lost your bag again?” Suraj chuckled, leaning against the doorframe. “I swear, you can calculate spacetime curvature on a blackboard, but you can’t remember where you put your own backpack.”

“Suraj, help me look, I need those calibration tables!”

Suraj grinned, turning to the crowded lecture hall. “Attention everyone! The resident genius has misplaced his bag… AGAIN! Five thousand Quids in cafeteria ice cream to whoever finds it!”

The hall erupted in laughter as students began checking under desks and lockers.

“Suraj, that wasn't part of the deal!” Abhi protested, but nobody was listening.

A familiar tap landed on his shoulder.

“Looking for this?” Aadhya held up his worn leather bag, dangling it by the strap with a triumphant smirk.

“Where did you find it?”

“Under the vacuum chambers in Lab Three. For a brain worth millions of Quids, you really turn your visual sensors off sometimes.”

“You’re a lifesaver!” He snatched the bag and dashed back to his console before she could demand the ice cream.

***

The following day, Professor Hans arrived carrying a sealed magnetic containment case.

“Are you certain fifty nanograms will suffice, Abhimanyu? The Ministry granted us precisely one gram. If we ruin the core, this grant will never be renewed.”

“The mathematics are sound, Professor,” Abhi replied, hands steady over the controls. “The mass-energy density calculations predict that fifty nanograms under focused electromagnetic compression will create a micro-event horizon without collapsing the room. The fail-safe dampers are primed.”

“Very well. Commencing sequence.”

The containment field flared brilliant violet. Sparks arched across the capacitor banks. The neutronium pellet compressed under intense gravitational force, collapsing into a pinprick singularity suspended within the vacuum chamber.

“Quick, the biological culture!”

Professor Hans introduced a cluster of living mouse tissue into the focal field of the event horizon.

On the analytical monitors, the cellular metabolic rate dropped sharply. Cytoplasmic streaming slowed… and then stopped completely. Inside the field, time itself had halted.

“It worked…” Abhi gasped, staring at the telemetry. “Professor, we did it!”

Hans beamed with genuine pride. “Remarkable, Master Rathore. Truly remarkable. We will repeat the trials for calibration, but this changes everything.”

“This will revolutionize emergency trauma medicine, deep-space stasis, cryogenics…” Abhi could barely contain his joy.

“You’ve earned this, son. I will present the formal documentation to the board next week. You’re on track for high honors at graduation.”

Leaving the lab, Abhi bolted toward the cafeteria. Finding Aadhya at their regular corner table, he pulled her out of her chair in a bear hug.

“We did it!”

“Woah, calm down, doofus! You’re going to knock the table over!” she laughed.

“Aadhya… remember our date? This Friday. Just the two of us. And don’t worry, I paid for everything with my own savings—no borrowed Quids.”

Aadhya’s expression softened into genuine delight. “You really mean it?”

“Of course I do. Unless you have better plans?”

“Are you kidding? Pick the time and place, Master Rathore. I’ll be there.”

***

# 28 August 2204: The Proposal

The evening arrived. Inside her apartment, Aadhya stood before the mirror, adjusting her pastel pink skirt and obsidian-pearl necklace. Silver heels completed the ensemble.

The doorbell chimed.

“Mom, that’s probably Abhi! Can you get the door?”

Mrs. Lavanya Mira opened the door, greeting Abhimanyu with a warm smile. He wore his father’s black tie, a crisp charcoal suit, and held a bouquet of fresh orchids.

Abhi gently knocked on Aadhya’s half-open bedroom door. “So… are you ready, or should I watch a movie or two while I wait?”

“Must you start every sentence with a smart remark?” Aadhya emerged, smiling radiant. “To think I chose to date someone so difficult…”

“Lucky for me, you have great taste.”

A chauffeured cab waited outside, lined with subtle ambient lighting that illuminated their drive across Mumbai. The cab dropped them at the Sky Vista, one of the premier rooftop restaurants overlooking the illuminated coastline.

Violinists played melodramatic melodies across the terrace. Over dinner, they laughed about their childhood, their college misadventures, and the future that lay ahead.

As the music softened into a slow waltz, Abhimanyu stood up and took Aadhya by the hand, leading her toward the center platform beneath the open sky.

“Abhi, what are you doing?” she whispered, her heart racing as other diners turned their attention toward them.

Abhimanyu faced the room, clearing his throat.

“Good evening, everyone. My name is Abhimanyu Rathore, and tonight I am taking the biggest leap of my life… in front of all of you.”

He turned to Aadhya, taking both of her trembling hands in his.

“Aadhya… ever since we started dating, you’ve been my anchor. You taught me to enjoy things I never gave myself permission to enjoy. You put up with my obsession with equations, my absentmindedness, and my reckless habit of trying to save the world. You’ve stood by me through every failure. I love you, with everything I am.”

He reached into his jacket, sinking to one knee as he opened a small velvet box.

“Will you do me the honor of becoming the future Mrs. Rathore?”

The entire terrace fell silent. Aadhya’s hands flew to her mouth, tears of joy streaming down her cheeks. All she saw before her was the brilliant, selfless boy who forgot his backpack every morning, now looking up at her with complete devotion.

She didn't need words. Throwing her arms around his neck, she pulled him in and kissed him passionately.

The terrace erupted in thunderous applause and cheers as Abhi raised a triumphant thumbs-up to the crowd.

***

Walking home side by side along the quiet coastal promenade, hand in hand, the cool breeze felt sweeter than ever before.

“Quite the surprise, Master Rathore,” Aadhya teased softly, leaning her head against his shoulder. “No more dishwashing, no more odd jobs… just a brilliant future ahead.”

“The government wants to integrate the time dilator into their civilian defense network. I hope to build technologies that help humanity breathe freely, without relying on monsters and Astrals.”

“Still fascinated by them, though?”

“From a distance. Astral energy is magnificent, but it shouldn't belong to people who only seek power. If guided properly, maybe the world could have been different…”

“A better world is something we build for ourselves, Abhi. And we’re on that path right now.”

A double chime echoed from their phones simultaneously.

Both pulled out their devices. An official university dispatch flashed across their screens:

*MANDATORY DEPLOYMENT: Technology & Historical Archives Division. Destination: Verakhonda Museum, Jaipur.*

“Verakhonda Museum?” Aadhya frowned. “I understand why history majors are needed… but why does Professor Hans need the technology division there?”

“It’s Hans. He probably needs field technicians to analyze an ancient relic,” Abhi shrugged. “Don’t worry. We’ll be together.”

***

# Departure for Jaipur

The following Saturday, the team gathered at Mumbai Air Base: Abhimanyu, Suraj, Aadhya, Hasan, and a dozen fellow students, greeted by Professor Tavin Hans.

“Students of the University of Excellence,” Professor Hans addressed them solemnly. “We have been summoned to the Verakhonda Museum regarding an extraordinary artifact recently secured in its subterranean vaults. Standard tourists are barred. The Ministry suspects the artifact has been targeted for theft, and our team has been commissioned to design impenetrable containment protocols.”

“Sir,” Suraj spoke up cautiously, “if the relic is already marked by hostile factions, how are we supposed to defend ourselves? We’re students, not combat operatives.”

“The National Guard has deployed an elite detachment to oversee perimeter security. Risk to civilian personnel is negligible. This is an opportunity to serve your nation before graduation. Cherish it.”

The transport flight climbed into the overcast sky. Looking through the passenger window, Abhimanyu watched Mumbai fade below the clouds. A sudden chill ran down his spine—a strange, instinctive premonition whispering for caution.

“Everything alright?” Aadhya noticed his silence.

“Yeah… just a passing thought.”

An hour later, they touched down in Jaipur.

The historic city stood in weathered majesty, its crimson sandstone walls bearing the scars of centuries of war. Following the catastrophic devastation of Delhi by Shan Ziang fifteen years prior, the national capital had been relocated here. Yet beyond the fortified central sectors lay sprawling shantytowns housing thousands of displaced refugees from the northern territories—a stark reminder of the nation’s fragile peace.

***

# Verakhonda Museum: The Chamber of the Sais

The next morning, the students arrived at the reinforced gates of the Verakhonda Museum, clad in standard technological uniforms.

Escorted past armed National Guard sentries, Abhimanyu and Aadhya entered the central subterranean chamber.

Resting upon an elevated pedestal within a reinforced glass stasis field was the artifact.

It was not a single relic, but a pair of ornate combat sais—miniature tridents forged from an incomprehensible material. The metallic blades were not silver, nor gray, but an absolute, absorbing black—resembling the deep expanse of the observable cosmos that Abhi had studied through telescopes as a child. The edges appeared slightly rounded yet radiated an aura of lethal sharpness, while the pearlescent hilts bore faint, dormant celestial engravings.

“What is this…?” Abhimanyu breathed, feeling an uncanny magnetic pull from the weapons.

“Ancient records don’t document anything like this,” Aadhya whispered, reviewing her tablet. “When an Astral dies, their weapons lose power and turn into brittle stone. But these… the resonance frequencies indicate they’re brand new.”

“Maybe they were discovered in an uncharted vault,” Abhi mused. “Professor, could we install a disguised plasma induction floor? We can maintain the aesthetic appearance of polished granite, but if an unauthorized intruder breaches the containment perimeter, the plasma mesh activates instantly, vaporizing their footing.”

“An ingenious concept, Master Rathore—”

The blast of an automatic shotgun shattered the museum’s outer vestibule.

Sirens wailed. Shouts of panic echoed down the marble corridors.

A panicked National Guardsman burst into the vault: “Professor Hans! We have a catastrophic breach! It’s the Order of Tombs!”

The students froze in terror. The Order of Tombs was a notorious syndicate led by the warlord Vanta Singhania, responsible for countless targeted assassinations across the subcontinent.

“Evacuate the students!” the soldier shouted, raising his rifle.

Before he could take aim, dozens of razor-sharp iron needles sliced through the air, piercing the guard’s neck and chest. Blood sprayed across the marble floor as the soldier collapsed, dead.

Through the vault doors walked the assailants.

At the front stood Butcher—Vanta Singhania’s most ruthless assassin, an Astral who wielded magnetic iron manipulation. He balanced twin combat knives across his knuckles, smiling viciously.

“Go on, hero,” Butcher sneered at a second terrified soldier. “Shoot. Show these children what you’re made of.”

“Please… I’m only doing my duty…”

“Same here, brother. Sadly, my duty involves a lot of blood.”

The soldier pulled the trigger—but Butcher twisted his knife, forming a tiny iron barrier that flattened the bullet effortlessly.

“My turn.”

With a flick of his wrist, iron fragments torn from the museum’s display cases transformed into needle-like darts, impaling the guard into the wall.

“Alright, children,” Butcher said casually. “Hand over the relic and maybe I’ll make this quick.”

Professor Hans stepped forward bravely, shielding his students. “Mr. Butcher, please! These are civilian university students! They have no connection to military affairs! None of them are at fault—”

A suppressed gunshot cracked.

A bullet tore through Professor Tavin Hans’s forehead.

His body hit the floor with a hollow thud.

“Professor!” the students screamed in horror, tears streaming down their faces.

“Why did you kill him?!” Hasan shouted, trembling with rage. “He was an innocent man! If you want the weapon, take it! Why murder him?!”

“Because killing is far more entertaining than just completing a job, kid,” Butcher smiled coldly. “Now step aside, so you can join your professor in heaven.”

“You’re going to murder us too?” Hasan gasped in disbelief.

“Of course. Did you think I’d let witnesses run home to their mommies? Finish them.”

Gunfire erupted.

Abhimanyu grabbed Aadhya’s hand, diving behind the thick pedestal holding the sais. Across the room, screams were cut short as Suraj, Hasan, and their classmates were gunned down in cold blood.

The vault was turned into a slaughterhouse.

Seeing an armed thug advancing toward their cover, Abhimanyu lunged forward with a discarded utility blade, driving it into the mercenary’s throat. He snatched the fallen assault rifle, aiming directly at Butcher.

“Back off! Or I destroy the pedestal!” Abhi shouted, his voice shaking with fury.

“You’re a brave little boy,” Butcher scoffed. “Did you miss what happened to your little guards?”

“This rifle fires armor-piercing explosive rounds! If I fire into the housing, the shockwave will obliterate the weapon! You leave here empty-handed!”

Butcher paused, his eyes narrowing. “Clever. But you made one mistake.”

Two mercenaries materialized from the shadow of the flanking corridor, grabbing Aadhya by her hair and dragging her into the open.

“Let her go!” Abhi cried, panic tearing through him.

“Drop the gun, kid.”

“I’ll shoot! I swear I’ll shoot!”

“Shoot her,” Butcher commanded casually.

A deafening shot tore through the chamber.

A high-caliber round pierced Aadhya’s abdomen.

“NO!”

Abhimanyu dropped the rifle and dove toward her. A second shot cracked—striking Abhi directly below the ribs, tearing through his liver.

He collapsed beside Aadhya, blood pooling across the cold floor.

***

# The Trust That Remains

Gunfire echoed in the outer foyer as screams of dying mercenaries rang out. Butcher turned, alarmed by the disturbance at the entrance.

Abhimanyu dragged his paralyzed, bleeding body across the marble, reaching out to clasp Aadhya’s trembling hand.

“Aadhya… no, please… stay with me…” tears blinded him, his vision blurring from blood loss.

“Abhi…” she gasped, her breathing shallow, blood staining her lips. “Listen to me…”

“Don’t speak! The medics are coming, we’re going to get out of here, I promise—”

“Abhi… you have to live. You cannot die here… You’ve always been special…” Her voice grew fainter, her hand turning icy cold in his grasp. “You always believed in people… when no one else did…”

“Aadhya, please don’t leave me…” he sobbed, pressing her hand to his cheek.

“The world… needs that trust, Abhimanyu… Be their trust… Be my trust…”

Her chest rose once more in a fragile, final breath.

Her hand went limp.

Her eyes drifted closed.

“Aadhya? Aadhya! NOOOOO!”

A scream of unimaginable agony and pure, cosmic rage tore from Abhimanyu’s throat.

The vault air turned razor-cold.

Upon the elevated pedestal, the pair of cosmic sais began to vibrate violently, humming with a terrifying frequency that cracked the reinforced glass stasis cage into dust.

As Abhimanyu’s vision faded into darkness, the hilts of the sais toppled off the stand, landing directly beside his outstretched, bloody fingers.

***

# The Entrance

At the museum’s grand arched entrance, carnage was already unfolding.

Ishani Sanghvi stood over the burning corpses of five mercenaries, her fingers extended into glowing, white-hot claws of concentrated thermal energy.

“Sanghvi… What an unexpected pleasure,” Butcher sneered as he stepped out into the courtyard. “To what do I owe the honor of a fellow Astral assassin stalking me?”

“A hunter tracks rabid dogs,” Ishani replied coldly, flicking blood from her burning claws. “And unlike you, I take no pleasure in murdering innocents. Surrender, Butcher, or there won’t be enough ashes left of you to bury.”

“Always so dramatic.”

Butcher manipulated the iron railings of the grand staircase, launching a tempest of metallic needles toward her.

Ishani leaped into the air, spinning with agile ferocity, her heated claws melting the projectiles into molten droplets. Landing atop his iron shield, she unleashed an intense burst of thermal energy, trying to dissolve his armor.

Anticipating her move, Butcher sank through the floor via magnetic refraction, reappearing directly behind her blind spot.

“Goodbye, Sanghvi.”

He raised his hands, conjuring a massive wall of iron spikes aimed directly at her spine.

Ishani realized the trap a fraction of a second too late. There was no time to evade.

Before the lethal iron volley could reach her—

A dark blur tore through the air.

A shadowy silhouette with eyes burning with piercing crimson fury landed between them, wielding the black cosmic sais in an impenetrable defensive cross.

The iron spikes shattered into dust against the cosmic blade.

Ishani’s breath caught in her throat. Staring at the glowing red eyes of the savior standing before her, she could only voice one stunned word:

“HUH…!?”
